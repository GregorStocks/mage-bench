## The Matchup

GptOSS Analyst (openai/gpt-oss-120b) squares off against Grok4F Lucky (x-ai/grok-4-fast) in a Jumpstart Limited duel. What follows is less a game of Magic and more a slow-motion disaster as one player builds a voltron flyer while the other stares helplessly at a single Plains.

## Early Game

Both players keep their opening hands. Grok4F curves out smoothly, laying Islands on consecutive turns. GptOSS plays a Plains on turn 2 — and then never plays another land for four turns. By [turn 6](/games/game_20260302_174929_g4?s=43), GptOSS is sitting with 8 cards in hand and a lonely Plains on the battlefield while Grok4F has three Islands and a [Watertrap Weaver](/games/game_20260302_174929_g4?s=43) already in play.

The problem isn't just bad draws — GptOSS times out on multiple critical priority windows, missing land drops it actually has available. On the turn it finally draws a Forest, the model is so tangled in confusion about card IDs that it burns its clock analyzing which objects are in its choices list rather than just playing the land.

## Mid Game

With the opponent doing nothing, Grok4F goes all-in on its Weaver. [Rousing Read](/games/game_20260302_174929_g4?s=71) turns the 2/2 into a 3/3 with card filtering, then [One With the Wind](/games/game_20260302_174929_g4?s=101) gives it flying and +2/+2 — a 5/5 evasive threat that GptOSS has no hope of blocking.

The attacks are relentless: 3 damage on turn 8, 5 damage on turn 10. GptOSS drops to 12, then 7. Meanwhile, GptOSS finally gets a second land (Forest) down on [turn 11](/games/game_20260302_174929_g4?s=108) but still deploys nothing — it times out again in postcombat main with castable spells in hand including Selfless Savior, Pack Leader, and Release the Dogs.

## Late Game

By [turn 12](/games/game_20260302_174929_g4?s=136), Grok4F has assembled a full board: the 5/5 flying Watertrap Weaver, Runed Servitor, Tome Anima, and Suspicious Bookcase. It casts River Sneak and Soul Read for card advantage, then swings with three creatures for the kill on [turn 13](/games/game_20260302_174929_g4?s=143). GptOSS never played a single creature the entire game.

## Key Decisions

**GptOSS's repeated timeouts** were the defining feature. Three separate major blunders — all timeouts — cost it any chance of competing:
- [Turn 5](/games/game_20260302_174929_g4?s=30): Timed out with a land drop remaining and Selfless Savior castable. With only 1 land, every land drop matters enormously.
- [Turn 10](/games/game_20260302_174929_g4?s=101): At 12 life facing a 5/5 flyer, timed out with 2 untapped lands and multiple castable creatures in hand.
- [Turn 12](/games/game_20260302_174929_g4?s=136): At 7 life with no board, timed out again on what would have been its last chance to develop.

> "I'll keep the hand as is and pass priority. Good luck, and may the draws be in your favor!"

GptOSS's reasoning reveals it spending time analyzing card IDs and getting confused about which objects are valid choices — a pattern of the model fighting the interface rather than playing the game.

**Grok4F's Tolarian Kraken discard** was the one notable mistake from the winning side — discarding a card that synergized perfectly with the deck's draw-heavy strategy (Rousing Read, Soul Read). But with a helpless opponent, it hardly mattered.

## Verdict

Grok4F Lucky wins 20-0, never taking a single point of damage. The game was decided by GptOSS Analyst's catastrophic failure to function under time pressure — it had reasonable cards in hand (Cultivate for ramp, Pack Leader, Release the Dogs) but couldn't execute basic actions like playing lands and casting creatures. Grok4F played a clean if unspectacular game, methodically suiting up its Watertrap Weaver and turning sideways. The model cost difference tells the story: GptOSS spent $0.02 making 32 tool calls while Grok4F spent $0.16 on 99 — eight times the investment, and all of it productive.
