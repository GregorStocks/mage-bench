# game_20260303_082623_g4

**Players**: MiniMx Worrier (minimax/minimax-m2.5, worrier) vs GPT5 Story (openai/gpt-5, storyteller)
**Format**: Jumpstart (Blink+Chandra vs Law+Think Again)
**Result**: MiniMx Worrier won — GPT5 Story ran out of time on turn 20 (23 life vs 18 life)
**Config**: round-robin-jumpstart
**Duration**: ~80 minutes (08:44-10:04), 60-minute match clock

## Findings

- **No code bugs.** The only platform issue — `get_action_choices` returning undeclared `current_step` field — was already fixed by commit 58accffd5d. No Java exceptions, no crashes, no bridge disconnects.
- **GPT5 Story lost by timer timeout**, not combat damage. GPT-5 spent 3240s of thinking time (94.9% reasoning tokens) and $3.28 across 134 LLM calls. Three 120-second LLM timeouts burned 6 minutes of clock. Extremely passive play: only 2 attacks in the entire 20-turn game.
- **GPT5 Story had 9 blunder annotations** (2 major, 3 moderate, 4 minor). Major: passed entire precombat main phase without action (T13); multiple timeout failures to declare blockers (T4, T11, T20). The final blocking failure (T20) had detailed reasoning planning optimal blocks but the model ran out of time before submitting them.
- **MiniMx Worrier had 6 blunder annotations** (1 major, 1 moderate, 4 minor). Major: cast Faith's Fetters (premium 4-mana removal) targeting its own Thriving Bluff — a complete waste. Also targeted own Brightmare with its ETB tap ability instead of opponent's Gideon's Lawkeeper.
- **MiniMx Worrier self-targeted with Hungry Flames**: dealt 2 damage to itself instead of the opponent. Game events confirm: "MiniMx Worrier casts Hungry Flames targeting Kami of Ancient Law, MiniMx Worrier." This is a model misplay, not a code bug.
- **minimax-m2.5 skipped `get_action_choices` before targeting** in 4 instances (auto-populating pattern). This directly caused the Faith's Fetters and Brightmare targeting blunders — the model guessed at target IDs without checking available targets. Known weak-model pattern (doc/investigating-game-logs.md "Blind index-0 targeting").
- **minimax-m2.5 parameter stuffing**: consistently fills all `choose_action` params (attackers, blockers, amounts, text, mana_plan) even when only `choice` is relevant. Bridge correctly ignores unused params. Not harmful but wasteful.
- **Worrier personality**: mild anxiety in chat ("That Midnight Guard looks scary"), no infection in gameplay decisions. Model's blunders are strategic misunderstandings, not personality-driven.
- **Storyteller personality**: moderate chat infection (all 6 messages are in-character narrative: "And lo, the Kami arrives — an omen"). Reasoning traces have ~24% narrative keywords but the infection is cosmetic — the model's strategic failures (bad combat math, extreme passivity) are inherent GPT-5 weaknesses, not personality-induced.
- **GPT5 stale board state tracking**: after timeouts and long waits, model referenced destroyed permanents. 2 "Object not found" errors, 1 "Unknown short ID" in mana_plan. Not a code bug — the model cached IDs from previous turns.
- **15 blunder annotations total** (9 GPT5 Story, 6 MiniMx Worrier). Breakdown: 2 major, 3 moderate, 9 minor, 1 questionable.

## Notes

- GPT-5 at reasoning_effort=medium is extremely slow and expensive ($3.28 / game) but still times out on decisions. The 94.9% reasoning token ratio means almost all output is internal thinking. Combined with 3 LLM timeouts, this consistently burns through the 60-minute clock. This is the same pattern seen in other GPT-5 games.
- minimax-m2.5 at $0.54 / game is cheap and fast (only 1 timeout, 8 thinking entries in 607 pilot log lines) but makes shallow strategic errors: self-targeting with Hungry Flames, Faith's Fetters on own land, Brightmare ETB on self. The "skip get_action_choices" pattern is a key contributor — the model makes blind decisions without checking available targets.
- The game was decided by clock management, not board state. GPT5 was at 18 life with Magnanimous Magistrate + Midnight Guard + multiple creatures when it timed out. MiniMx had just cast Flames of the Firebrand to weaken the board and attacked with 3 creatures for the timeout win.
- Hungry Flames self-targeting is a recurring minimax-m2.5 pattern worth monitoring across games. The model may not understand multi-target spells that require "target creature AND target player."
