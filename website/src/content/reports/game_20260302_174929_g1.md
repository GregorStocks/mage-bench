## The Matchup

QwCdr Proud (Qwen3 Coder) brings a RG creature deck with Crawling Sensation, Sethron, Hurloon General, and Nantuko Cultivator. Llama4 Rules (Llama 4 Maverick) runs GW with Turntimber Basilisk, Canopy Baloth, and removal spells including Divine Verdict and Valorous Stance. The tool call stats tell a story before the game even starts: Llama4 has 29 failed tool calls to QwCdr's 3.

## Early Game

Both players develop slowly. Llama4 leads with [Oashra Cultivator](/games/game_20260302_174929_g1?s=33) on turn 5 — a 0/3 that can fetch a land. QwCdr answers with [Duskshell Crawler](/games/game_20260302_174929_g1?s=48) on turn 6 and [Ironshell Beetle](/games/game_20260302_174929_g1?s=96) on turn 8, placing a +1/+1 counter on the Crawler. Llama4 adds [Midnight Guard](/games/game_20260302_174929_g1?s=72) and [Turntimber Basilisk](/games/game_20260302_174929_g1?s=118) for deathtouch pressure.

The early trades are reasonable but Llama4 shows its confusion early — it attacks with the 0/3 Oashra Cultivator, dealing zero damage while tapping the creature for no benefit.

## Mid Game

The game develops into a scrappy creature brawl. On [turn 11](/games/game_20260302_174929_g1?s=165), Llama4 has a clear [lethal attack](/games/game_20260302_174929_g1?s=165): opponent at 14 life with all creatures tapped. Turntimber Basilisk (2) + Canopy Baloth (4/3 that entered this turn) + Kami of Ancient Law (2) + Oashra Cultivator (0) could swing with Evolving Wilds activating Basilisk's landfall. But Llama4 only sends the Basilisk, dealing a paltry 2 damage.

QwCdr capitalizes, landing [Crawling Sensation](/games/game_20260302_174929_g1?s=219) on turn 13 which starts generating Insect tokens for free. The enchantment mills cards and creates 1/1s whenever a land hits the graveyard — slow but inevitable value.

## Late Game

The catastrophic sequence happens on [turn 17](/games/game_20260302_174929_g1?s=321). Llama4 attacks with Canopy Baloth (temporarily 6/5 from landfall) and Turntimber Basilisk. The opponent has no untapped blockers. Victory should be straightforward. Instead, Llama4 casts [Divine Verdict](/games/game_20260302_174929_g1?s=321) — a spell that destroys an attacking or blocking creature — *during its own combat phase*. With no blockers declared, the only legal targets are Llama4's own attackers.

It gets worse. Llama4 has Valorous Stance in hand, which could grant indestructible to save the targeted creature. It times out without casting it. Then, forced to choose which of its own creatures to sacrifice, it picks the 6/5 Canopy Baloth over the 2/1 Basilisk — killing its biggest threat and dealing only 2 damage instead of 8.

On [turn 18](/games/game_20260302_174929_g1?s=350), QwCdr drops [Sethron, Hurloon General](/games/game_20260302_174929_g1?s=350), a 4/4 that creates Minotaur tokens. It swings for 7 damage with four creatures, and Llama4 times out making no blocks despite having available defenders. Llama4 drops to 2 life. A final alpha strike on [turn 19](/games/game_20260302_174929_g1?s=374) with five creatures — including the newly cast Borderland Minotaur — finishes the game.

## Key Decisions

**Llama4's self-Divine Verdict**: The defining blunder. Casting creature removal during your own combat when the opponent has no blockers can only target your own creatures. Three compounding failures — casting the wrong spell, timing out on the response window, then sacrificing the better creature — turned a winning position into a losing one.

**Llama4's missed lethal on turn 15**: With the opponent at 10 life and all creatures tapped, attacking with everything would have dealt at least 10. Instead, only Turntimber Basilisk and Canopy Baloth attacked for 8, leaving the opponent alive at 2.

**QwCdr's Sethron deployment**: Landing a 4/4 that generates tokens when Llama4 is at 9 life with a depleted board was the killing blow. QwCdr also correctly used Arrest on Sethron (when Llama4 had it) but the Arrest didn't prevent triggered abilities.

## Verdict

QwCdr Proud wins despite making several moderate mistakes of its own (Thriving Grove color timeout, questionable blocks). The game was Llama4's to lose, and lose it did — spectacularly. The 29 failed tool calls, repeated timeouts, and the unforgettable self-Divine Verdict paint a picture of a model struggling fundamentally with the game interface. QwCdr's steadier execution and incremental value from Crawling Sensation and Sethron carried it through a game that could have gone either way with competent play from both sides.
