## The Matchup

Grok-4 Fast pilots a blue-red deck with Lightning Visionary, Shambling Suit, Filigree Attendant, and Homing Lightning — an artifact-matters shell with efficient removal. Mistral Large brings a red-heavy deck headlined by Chandra, Heart of Fire, backed by Chandra's Magmutt and Unstable Obelisk. On paper, the planeswalker gives Mistral Large a real card advantage engine. In practice, it never uses it properly.

## Early Sparring

Mistral Large opens on the play with [Chandra's Magmutt on turn 3](/games/game_20260302_184350_g3?s=28), a 2/2 hound that can ping for 1 damage each turn. Grok-4 Fast responds with [Lightning Visionary](/games/game_20260302_184350_g3?s=40) on turn 4. MstLg Trash's first minor misstep comes immediately: rather than attacking with the Magmutt into the summoning-sick Visionary (forcing either a trade or 2 damage), it taps the Magmutt to ping for just 1. A small leak, but a sign of things to come.

Unstable Obelisk arrives on turn 5, giving Mistral Large ramp and a potential removal spell. Grok-4 Fast [swings with Lightning Visionary for 2](/games/game_20260302_184350_g3?s=75) and deploys Shambling Suit, a creature that grows based on artifact count.

## Chandra Arrives — Then Burns Out

[Turn 7 is the pivotal moment](/games/game_20260302_184350_g3?s=97). MstLg Trash slams Chandra, Heart of Fire, a powerful planeswalker that can generate card advantage by exiling the top three cards of the library. But then it immediately activates Chandra's +1 with all lands tapped, discarding its hand — including Chandra's Incinerator, a 6/6 trampler — for three exiled cards it can't cast because it has no mana. The entire hand is thrown away for nothing.

Grok-4 Fast punishes this sharply. It fires a [Lightning Bolt at Chandra](/games/game_20260302_184350_g3?s=118), knocking her from 5 to 2 loyalty, then attacks with both creatures to push MstLg Trash to 14 life.

## The Forgotten Planeswalker

What follows is arguably worse than the initial misplay. MstLg Trash enters [turn 9 with an empty hand](/games/game_20260302_184350_g3?s=129) and Chandra at 3 loyalty. Chandra's +1 with an empty hand is essentially a free "draw three" — you discard nothing and exile three cards to play. It's the strongest possible use of the ability. MstLg Trash passes the turn without activating it.

[Turn 11, the same thing happens again.](/games/game_20260302_184350_g3?s=150) Empty hand, Chandra available, three untapped lands plus Unstable Obelisk to cast whatever gets exiled. MstLg Trash passes without using it. Two consecutive turns of free card advantage thrown away while falling behind on board. These are the game's two major blunders, and they're identical — the model simply forgets its planeswalker exists.

MstLg Trash does eventually use Chandra's +1 on turn 12, but by then the damage is done.

## Grok-4 Closes It Out

Grok-4 Fast steadily builds its board: [Filigree Attendant on turn 10](/games/game_20260302_184350_g3?s=150), [Living Lightning on turn 12](/games/game_20260302_184350_g3?s=187). When MstLg Trash deploys a second Chandra's Magmutt as a last line of defense, Grok-4 Fast has the perfect answer: [Homing Lightning](/games/game_20260302_184350_g3?s=202), which deals 4 damage to a creature and all other creatures sharing its name. Both Magmutts die simultaneously, clearing the path for a four-creature alpha strike that ends the game.

## Key Decisions

**Chandra's wasted +1 (Turn 7)** — Activating Chandra's discard-and-exile ability with no mana to spend on exiled cards turned a card advantage engine into a discard outlet. Chandra's Incinerator was among the casualties.

**Two missed Chandra activations (Turns 9 and 11)** — With an empty hand, using Chandra's +1 costs literally nothing and provides up to three playable cards. Missing this twice in a row cost Mistral Large roughly six cards of potential advantage — an insurmountable deficit.

**Homing Lightning timing** — Grok-4 Fast waited until both Magmutts were on the battlefield to get maximum value from Homing Lightning's name-matching clause. Clean, patient execution.

## Verdict

Grok-4 Fast wins 13 to 0 in a 14-turn game, playing a disciplined game with well-timed removal. MstLg Trash's repeated failures to activate its own planeswalker — the strongest card on either side of the table — turned what could have been a competitive match into a slow bleed. Mistral Large's reasoning was coherent for basic plays but showed no awareness of activated abilities on its own permanents, a significant gap in board state tracking.
